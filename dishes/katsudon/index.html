カツ丼
Katsudon · kah-tsoo-dohn

What is katsudon?

Katsudon is a deep-fried breaded pork cutlet simmered with egg and onion in sweet soy-dashi sauce, served over rice. It is hearty, mild and one of the most popular rice bowls in Japan.

Quick answer: Katsudon is tonkatsu (pork cutlet) simmered in egg-and-dashi sauce over rice. It contains pork, egg, wheat (the breading) and soy, and the sauce often uses fish dashi.

What is in a katsudon bowl?

A pork cutlet (tonkatsu) is breaded in flour, egg and panko and deep-fried. For katsudon it is sliced, simmered briefly with onion in a sauce of dashi, soy sauce and mirin, then topped with beaten egg that sets softly over rice. It is not spicy and not raw. Some shops serve a set with miso soup and pickles.

Why people eat it before exams

The word katsu sounds like katsu (勝つ), “to win”, so students and athletes eat katsudon for luck. You will see it on menus at casual restaurants, teishoku shops and chains such as Katsuya and Matsunoya. Order it like oyakodon: by name or by pointing; compare it with oyakodon.

Allergy notes

Katsudon has several allergens at once: egg (in both the breading and the topping), wheat (flour and panko), soy (soy sauce) and often fish (dashi). If you cannot have egg, ask for sōsu katsudon (ソースカツ丼), a Fukui-style sauce-only version with no egg topping, but the breading may still contain egg. See our egg allergy guide and gluten-free guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is katsudon raw?

No. The cutlet is deep-fried and the egg sets in the hot sauce to a soft, scrambled texture.

Is katsudon spicy?

No. It is sweet and savory, based on dashi, soy sauce and mirin.

Is there egg in katsudon?

Yes, twice: the breading usually uses egg, and the topping is beaten egg. Ask about sōsu katsudon if you avoid the topping.

Is katsudon the same as oyakodon?

They share the sauce and egg, but oyakodon uses simmered chicken, while katsudon uses a fried pork cutlet.
