Vegetarian and vegan in Japan: a restaurant card and what to order

JapanMust Guides · 5 min read · Updated November 2026

Japan has tofu, pickles, rice and seasonal vegetables at almost every meal, so vegetarians expect an easy trip. The catch is dashi (だし), a stock made from dried bonito fish. It flavors miso soup, noodle broths, simmered vegetables and many sauces, and staff often do not think of it as “fish”. Vegans also need to watch egg, which is common in batters and sauces.

Quick answer: Say 肉と魚とだしが食べられません (niku to sakana to dashi ga taberaremasen) and show the card below. Ask if the stock can be 昆布だし (kombu dashi, seaweed stock). For a fully plant-based meal, look for 精進料理 (shoujin ryouri).

Where meat and fish hide

  • Dashi. Miso soup, udon and soba broth, oden, simmered vegetables (nimono), rolled omelette and the dipping sauce for tempura all usually contain bonito stock. See our dashi guide.
  • Bonito flakes on top. Katsuobushi (かつお節) is sprinkled on okonomiyaki, takoyaki, cold tofu and spinach side dishes.
  • Ramen. Nearly all ramen broths use pork, chicken or fish. Even “vegetable” ramen may use an animal stock unless the shop is specifically vegan.
  • Curry. Japanese curry roux often contains beef or pork fat and meat extract. See what is in Japanese curry.
  • “Salad”. Potato salad and many side salads include ham, bacon or mayonnaise (egg).
  • Vegan extras. Egg in tempura batter, noodles and sweets; milk and butter in bread, curry and Western-style desserts.

What usually works

  • Shojin ryori (精進料理): Buddhist temple cuisine, traditionally free of meat, fish, egg and dairy. Kyoto and Mount Koya are famous for it.
  • Vegetable sushi: kappa maki (cucumber roll), oshinko maki (pickle roll), nattō maki and avocado rolls. Soy sauce is plant-based.
  • Onigiri with umeboshi (pickled plum) from a konbini, checking the label for 魚介 or かつお.
  • Zaru soba or udon with the dipping sauce left out. Bring your own soy sauce or ask for salt.
  • Specialty shops: vegan ramen and curry shops exist in Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka. Search “vegan ramen” in your map app before you go.

Printable vegetarian and vegan card

Show this before you order. It lists exactly what you avoid, which works better than the word “vegetarian” alone.

ベジタリアン · VEGETARIAN

私はベジタリアンです。

I am vegetarian.

肉（牛・豚・鶏）、魚、えび・かに・貝は食べられません。

I cannot eat meat (beef, pork, chicken), fish or shellfish.

かつおだし、煮干し、かつお節、肉や魚のエキス・スープも食べられません。

I also cannot eat bonito stock, dried sardines, bonito flakes, or meat or fish extract or broth.

昆布だしや野菜だしなら大丈夫です。

Kombu (seaweed) or vegetable stock is fine.

ヴィーガンなので、卵・牛乳・バター・チーズ・はちみつも食べられません。

I am vegan, so I also cannot eat egg, milk, butter, cheese or honey.

ご協力ありがとうございます。 Thank you for your help. · japanmust.com

Frequently asked questions

Is it hard to be vegetarian in Japan?

It is harder than it looks. Vegetables are everywhere, but most savory dishes are built on dashi, a stock made from bonito fish flakes. A card that says “no meat, no fish, no fish stock” solves most of the problem.

Is miso soup vegetarian?

Usually not. Restaurant miso soup is almost always made with bonito dashi. Ask for kombu (seaweed) dashi, or skip it.

Will Japanese staff understand “vegetarian”?

ベジタリアン (bejitarian) is widely understood, but some people think only of red meat and still serve fish stock, ham or bacon. List exactly what you do not eat.

Where can vegans eat in Japan?

Look for shojin ryori (精進料理), Buddhist temple cuisine, which is traditionally plant-based. Big cities also have vegan ramen and curry shops, and apps like HappyCow list them.
