How to read a Japanese menu

JapanMust Guides · 6 min read

Short answer: about 30 kanji cover most menus — find the protein, the cooking method, and the price. You don't need to learn Japanese to decode a menu. About 30 kanji and patterns cover the vast majority of what you'll see. Learn to spot these and you can get through nearly any menu in 60 seconds.

Proteins — the main event

  • 肉 (niku) — meat
  • 牛肉 (gyuuniku) — beef · 豚肉 (butaniku) — pork · 鶏肉 (toriniku) — chicken
  • 魚 (sakana) — fish · 鮭 (shake) — salmon · まぐろ (maguro) — tuna
  • えび (ebi) — shrimp · たこ (tako) — octopus · いか (ika) — squid
  • 卵 (tamago) — egg

Cooking methods

  • 焼 (yaki) — grilled · 揚 (age) — deep-fried · 煮 (ni) — simmered
  • 蒸 (mushi) — steamed · 生 (nama) — raw / draft (as in 生ビール draft beer)
  • 刺身 (sashimi) — sliced raw fish

Menu structure words

  • 定食 (teishoku) — set meal (main + rice + miso + pickles, great value)
  • 丼 (don) — rice bowl · 麺 (men) — noodles
  • 大盛り (oomori) — large portion · 小 (shou) / 大 (dai) — small / large
  • おすすめ (osusume) — recommendation · 日替わり (higawari) — daily special
  • 税込 (zeikomi) — tax included

The 60-second decode method

  1. Find the protein — scan for 肉・魚・鶏・豚・牛・えび. That tells you what you're eating.
  2. Find the method — 焼・揚げ・煮・刺身 tells you how it's cooked.
  3. Check the format — 丼 (bowl), 定食 (set), 麺 (noodles).
  4. Check the price — numbers are universal. ¥800–1,200 is a normal lunch.

Example: 鶏の唐揚げ定食 ¥900 → chicken (鶏） + deep-fried (唐揚げ） + set meal （定食） = fried chicken set for ¥900. You just read a menu.

Quick answers

How many kanji do I need to read a Japanese menu?

About 30 kanji and patterns cover the vast majority of what you'll see. Learn to spot these and you can get through nearly any menu in 60 seconds.

How do I decode a Japanese menu in 60 seconds?

Find the protein — scan for 肉, 魚, 鶏, 豚, 牛, or えび. Find the method — 焼, 揚げ, 煮, or 刺身. Check the format — 丼 for a bowl, 定食 for a set meal, 麺 for noodles. Then check the price: numbers are universal, and ¥800–1,200 is a normal lunch.

What does 定食 (teishoku) mean on a Japanese menu?

A set meal — a main dish plus rice, miso soup, and pickles. It's great value, and one of the most useful words to recognize: look for 定食 when you want a complete meal at a fixed price.
