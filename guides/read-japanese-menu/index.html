How to read a Japanese menu

JapanMust Guides · 6 min read

You don't need to learn Japanese to decode a menu. About 30 kanji and patterns cover the vast majority of what you'll see. Learn to spot these and you can get through nearly any menu in 60 seconds.

Proteins — the main event

  • 肉 (niku) — meat
  • 牛肉 (gyuuniku) — beef · 豚肉 (butaniku) — pork · 鶏肉 (toriniku) — chicken
  • 魚 (sakana) — fish · 鮭 (shake) — salmon · まぐろ (maguro) — tuna
  • えび (ebi) — shrimp · たこ (tako) — octopus · いか (ika) — squid
  • 卵 (tamago) — egg

Cooking methods

  • 焼 (yaki) — grilled · 揚 (age) — deep-fried · 煮 (ni) — simmered
  • 蒸 (mushi) — steamed · 生 (nama) — raw / draft (as in 生ビール draft beer)
  • 刺身 (sashimi) — sliced raw fish

Menu structure words

  • 定食 (teishoku) — set meal (main + rice + miso + pickles, great value)
  • 丼 (don) — rice bowl · 麺 (men) — noodles
  • 大盛り (oomori) — large portion · 小 (shou) / 大 (dai) — small / large
  • おすすめ (osusume) — recommendation · 日替わり (higawari) — daily special
  • 税込 (zeikomi) — tax included

The 60-second decode method

  1. Find the protein — scan for 肉・魚・鶏・豚・牛・えび. That tells you what you're eating.
  2. Find the method — 焼・揚げ・煮・刺身 tells you how it's cooked.
  3. Check the format — 丼 (bowl), 定食 (set), 麺 (noodles).
  4. Check the price — numbers are universal. ¥800–1,200 is a normal lunch.

Example: 鶏の唐揚げ定食 ¥900 → chicken (鶏） + deep-fried (唐揚げ） + set meal （定食） = fried chicken set for ¥900. You just read a menu.

When kanji isn't enough

Dish names (like 親子丼 or 肉じゃが） won't decode this way — they're vocabulary, not patterns. That's what dish explanations are for: see our dishes section for what each named dish actually is.
