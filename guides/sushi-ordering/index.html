Sushi ordering guide

JapanMust Guides · 5 min read

There are three completely different ways to eat sushi in Japan, and they work nothing alike. Pick your format first — everything else follows.

1. Kaiten （回転） — conveyor-belt sushi

The easiest entry point. Plates circulate on a belt; grab what looks good. Plate colors = prices (check the chart on the table). Modern chains use touch panels with an English button — order and it arrives by mini train.

  • Stack finished plates; staff count them at the end.
  • Wasabi and ginger are at the table; soy sauce dishes too.
  • Great chains: Sushiro, Kura Sushi, Hama Sushi. Lunch for ¥1,000–1,500.

2. Counter sushi — order piece by piece

At a regular sushi counter you order one or two pieces at a time ("hitotsu" / "futatsu"). Point at the display case or the menu. Good starter order:

  • まぐろ maguro — tuna · サーモン saamon — salmon
  • えび ebi — shrimp · たまご tamago — sweet egg
  • いくら ikura — salmon roe · うに uni — sea urchin (try it once)
まぐろを二つお願いします
Maguro o futatsu onegai shimasu — "Two tuna, please"

3. Omakase （おまかせ) — chef's choice

"I leave it to you." The chef serves a course of 10–20 pieces. Rules: book ahead, arrive on time, eat each piece right when served, don't ask for substitutions. Prices run ¥5,000–30,000+. For a first trip, a lunch omakase (¥3,000–6,000) is the sweet spot.

Etiquette that matters

  • Eat nigiri in one bite if you can.
  • Dip the fish side in soy sauce, not the rice — rice soaks up too much and falls apart.
  • Mixing wasabi into soy sauce is a tourist tell; the chef already put wasabi in the nigiri.
  • Ginger （がり gari) is a palate cleanser between pieces, not a topping.
