How to ask for the bill in Japanese

JapanMust Guides · 4 min read

In Japan the bill rarely comes automatically — you ask for it. One phrase handles nearly every situation:

お会計お願いします
Okaikei onegai shimasu — "Check, please"

Say it with eye contact and a small raised hand. At casual places, the shorter "Okaikei!" works fine.

Where do you pay?

  • Ramen shops / fast food: ticket machine or register — you already paid or pay at the counter.
  • Izakaya / nicer restaurants: usually at the register near the entrance, not at the table. Staff will direct you: "reji de onegai shimasu".
  • Cafes: either at the table or the register — look for a small tray or follow other customers.

Bills are often delivered on a small tray. Put your cash or card on the tray, not directly in their hand.

10 things staff say to you, decoded

  • いらっしゃいませ (irasshaimase) — "Welcome!" (no reply needed)
  • 何名様ですか？ (nan-mei sama desu ka?) — "How many people?" → hold up fingers
  • お決まりですか？ (okimari desu ka?) — "Have you decided?" → nod, or "mou sukoshi" （もう少し — "a little more time")
  • ご注文は？ (go-chuumon wa?) — "Your order?"
  • 以上でよろしいですか？ (ijou de yoroshii desu ka?) — "Is that everything?"
  • 少々お待ちください (shoushou omachi kudasai) — "One moment please"
  • お待たせしました (omatase shimashita) — "Sorry to keep you waiting" (food's here)
  • お会計はレジでお願いします — "Please pay at the register"
  • ありがとうございました (arigatou gozaimashita) — "Thank you" (as you leave)
  • またお越しください — "Please come again"

Money notes

  • No tipping. Leaving extra money confuses people; they may chase you to return it.
  • Cash is still king at small shops — carry yen. Many ticket machines don't take cards.
  • Tax is usually included in the displayed price at restaurants now （税込 zeikomi).
