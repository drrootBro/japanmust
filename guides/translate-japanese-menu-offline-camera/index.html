How to translate a Japanese menu with your camera, offline

JapanMust Guides · 5 min read · Updated November 2026

Pointing your camera at a Japanese menu is the fastest way to find out what is on it. The step many people miss is checking the result: a literal translation of a dish name can mislead, and an allergen can slip through. Here is a workflow that works without signal.

Quick answer: Set up offline language resources first, photograph the menu in good light one section at a time, then check allergen words in the original Japanese yourself. Confirm anything that matters with staff and a card.

Getting a clean scan

  1. Light and angle. Hold the phone parallel to the menu, avoid glare, and capture one section at a time.
  2. Zoom in on dishes rather than the whole page, so the text stays sharp.
  3. Know the limits. Handwritten menus, stylized fonts and vertical text (縦書き, tategaki) are harder to read. Use the pointing method if the scan fails.
  4. Take a photo for later and ask “写真を撮ってもいいですか？” first if the place is small.

Literal translation vs what the dish is

A word-for-word translation of 親子丼 gives “parent and child bowl”. A helpful tool also tells you it is a chicken and egg rice bowl; see what is oyakodon for the example. For dishes you do not recognize, our menu reading guide gives you kanji to spot on your own: 焼 (grilled), 揚 (fried), 煮 (simmered), 丼 (bowl), 定食 (set meal).

Check allergens in the Japanese text

Translations can omit or soften an ingredient. If you have an allergy, scan the original text for the words in the table below, and when in doubt show a card. Never rely on a translation or a menu description alone for a serious allergy; ask the staff to confirm with the kitchen. For offline setup details, see Japan offline translator.

Printable “which dishes are safe?” card

After you scan, show this card and point to the dish you are considering. Write your allergen in the blank.

私は（　　　　　　　　）アレルギーです。

I am allergic to (write your allergen here).

Frequently asked questions

Can I translate a Japanese menu offline?

Yes, if your translator supports on-device camera translation and you have downloaded the Japanese resources in advance. Test it in airplane mode.

Why does the translation sound strange?

Dish names are vocabulary, not sentences. A literal translation of 親子丼 gives “parent and child bowl”. Look for a tool that explains the dish.

Can the camera detect allergens?

It can help you read allergen words, but a menu description never guarantees what is in the food. Always confirm with staff, especially for a serious allergy.

What if the menu is handwritten?

Handwriting is hard for text recognition. Point at the item, ask staff, or use a short phrase and a card.
