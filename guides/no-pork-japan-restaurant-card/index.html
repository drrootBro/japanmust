No pork in Japan: where pork hides and a restaurant card

JapanMust Guides · 4 min read · Updated November 2026

Whether you avoid pork for religious reasons, kosher practice, health or a pork allergy, Japan takes some care. Pork is the everyday meat here: it fills gyoza, makes the broth of tonkotsu ramen, coats the cutlet in katsu, and lard is a common frying fat. The good news is that staff are used to the question, and chicken, beef and fish dishes are everywhere.

Quick answer: Say 豚肉が食べられません (butaniku ga taberaremasen), and ask スープに豚骨は入っていますか？ about any broth. Show the card below; it covers lard, pork extract and gelatin too.

Dishes that usually contain pork

  • Ramen. Tonkotsu is pork-bone broth; most ramen of any kind is topped with chashu pork and many broths blend in pork bones.
  • Gyoza, shumai and nikuman steamed buns.
  • Katsudon, tonkatsu (とんかつ) and katsu curry: the cutlet is pork unless it says チキン (chicken).
  • Okonomiyaki and yakisoba: pork belly is the standard filling.
  • Curry rice. Eastern Japan often uses pork, and roux blocks may contain lard or pork extract.
  • Soups and stews. Tonjiru (豚汁, pork miso soup), nikujaga in eastern Japan, and many hot pots.
  • Yakitori shops. Mostly chicken, but butabara (pork belly) skewers and bacon-wrapped skewers are common.

Hidden pork

  • ラード (raado): lard, in fried rice, some gyoza and fried foods.
  • ポークエキス (pooku ekisu): pork extract, in instant noodles, sauces and konbini food.
  • ゼラチン (zerachin): gelatin, in jelly desserts, gummies and some yogurts.
  • ハム・ベーコン・ソーセージ: ham, bacon and sausage in salads, sandwiches and breakfast sets.

Printable no-pork card

豚肉 NG · NO PORK

私は豚肉が食べられません。

I cannot eat pork.

豚骨スープ、チャーシュー、ハム、ベーコン、ソーセージも食べられません。

I also cannot eat pork-bone broth, chashu, ham, bacon or sausage.

ラード、ポークエキス、ゼラチンなど豚由来の材料が入っていないか、ご確認をお願いします。

Please check for pork-based ingredients such as lard, pork extract and gelatin.

鶏肉・牛肉・魚は大丈夫です。

Chicken, beef and fish are fine.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a lot of pork in Japanese food?

Yes, more than many visitors expect. Pork is the most common meat in ramen, gyoza, katsu, fried rice and eastern Japanese curry, and lard is used for frying.

Does “meat” in Japan mean pork?

Often. In eastern Japan, a dish simply called 肉 (niku, meat), like nikujaga or niku-udon, is often pork, while in western Japan it is more often beef. Always ask which meat.

Is chicken ramen safe if I avoid pork?

Not always. Chicken-broth (鶏白湯, tori paitan) ramen still usually comes with pork chashu, and some shops blend pork and chicken bones. Ask about both the broth and toppings.

Do Japanese labels show pork?

Pork (豚肉) is a recommended allergen item, so most packaged food lists it, but it is not mandatory. Also check the ingredients for ポーク, ラード and ゼラチン.
