Sesame allergy in Japan: where sesame hides and a restaurant card

JapanMust Guides · 4 min read · Updated November 2026

Sesame is one of the harder allergies to manage in Japan. It is not an exotic ingredient here: toasted sesame oil, white and black sesame seeds and creamy sesame paste turn up in dressings, side dishes, noodles and sweets. And because sesame is only a recommended label item in Japan, not a mandatory one, you cannot rely on packaging alone.

Quick answer: Say ごまアレルギーです (goma arerugii desu), show the card below, and always ask about ごま油 (goma abura, sesame oil). Skip goma dressing, shichimi pepper and anything described as goma.

Where sesame shows up in Japan

  • Dressings and sauces. Creamy goma dressing (ごまドレッシング) is the default on many salads, and goma-dare (ごまだれ) is the standard dip for shabu-shabu and cold noodles.
  • Side dishes. Goma-ae (ごま和え), usually spinach or green beans in sesame paste, appears in bento, set meals and izakaya menus.
  • Ramen and Chinese-style dishes. Tantanmen, hiyashi chuka, gyoza and fried rice often use sesame oil, seeds or paste. Some tonkotsu shops add ma-yu (black garlic sesame oil).
  • Tempura. Traditional tempura shops may fry in sesame oil or a sesame blend.
  • Toppings. Sesame seeds on rice, onigiri and sushi rolls; furikake rice seasoning; and shichimi togarashi (七味唐辛子), the seven-spice shaker on every noodle counter.
  • Temple food and sweets. Goma-dofu (胡麻豆腐), sesame “tofu”, is a staple of temple cuisine. Black sesame ice cream, mochi and buns are common desserts.

Labels: why “no sesame listed” is not enough

Japanese packaged food must declare eight major allergens, including peanut, egg, milk and wheat. Sesame sits on the second list of “recommended” items, so manufacturers are encouraged but not required to show it. Many do, written as ごま in the allergen line, but always scan the full ingredient list for ごま, 胡麻, ごま油 and 練りごま. Our allergen label guide shows where to look.

アレルギー · ALLERGY

私はごま（胡麻）アレルギーです。

I am allergic to sesame.

ごま、ごま油、練りごま、ごまドレッシングが入っている料理は食べられません。

I cannot eat dishes with sesame seeds, sesame oil, sesame paste or sesame dressing.

七味唐辛子やふりかけにも入っていないか、ご確認をお願いします。

Please also check seven-spice pepper and rice seasonings.

I also cannot eat food fried or stir-fried in sesame oil.

少量でも命に関わります。

Even a small amount can be life-threatening.

Allergy safety: this is travel information, not medical advice. Restaurants often cannot guarantee an allergen-free meal, and cross-contact in kitchens is common. Carry any prescribed medication and a doctor’s letter, and check current Japanese rules for bringing medicine into the country.

Frequently asked questions

Is sesame common in Japanese food?

Yes. Sesame seeds, sesame oil and sesame paste are everyday ingredients in Japan, from salad dressing and spinach side dishes to ramen, gyoza and tempura frying oil.

Do Japanese food labels have to list sesame?

Not always. Sesame (ごま) is on Japan’s list of recommended allergens, not the mandatory list, so a package may contain sesame without saying so in the allergen line. Read the full ingredient list too.

Is tempura fried in sesame oil?

Some traditional tempura shops, especially Tokyo-style ones, fry in sesame oil or a blend that includes it. Ask “揚げ油にごま油を使っていますか？” before ordering.

Does shichimi togarashi contain sesame?

Usually yes. The red seven-spice shaker on ramen, udon and yakitori counters normally includes sesame seeds. Ask for no topping and keep the shaker away.
