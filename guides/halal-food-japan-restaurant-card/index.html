Halal food in Japan: a restaurant card for Muslim travelers

JapanMust Guides · 5 min read · Updated November 2026

Japanese cooking does not center on pork the way some cuisines do, but pork and alcohol are both easy to miss. Pork bones make tonkotsu ramen broth, lard goes into fried rice and gyoza, and mirin and cooking sake sweeten everyday sauces. Most staff will happily help once they understand exactly what you avoid.

Quick answer: Say ムスリムです。豚肉とお酒が食べられません (musurimu desu. butaniku to osake ga taberaremasen), show the card below, and ask about みりん (mirin) and 料理酒 (cooking sake). In big cities, search for “halal” restaurants before you go.

Where pork hides

  • Ramen. Tonkotsu broth is pork bone, most ramen comes with chashu pork slices, and even shoyu or miso broths often use pork bones.
  • Gyoza and buns. Standard gyoza and nikuman (steamed buns) are pork-filled.
  • Katsu and curry. Tonkatsu and katsudon are pork cutlet, and curry roux often contains pork fat or extract.
  • Hidden forms. ラード (lard), ポークエキス (pork extract), ゼラチン (gelatin) in sweets, and ham or bacon in salads and sandwiches.

Our no-pork guide has a longer list of dishes.

Where alcohol hides

  • Mirin (みりん): sweet rice wine in teriyaki, sukiyaki, simmered fish and noodle dipping sauces.
  • Cooking sake (料理酒, ryourishu): in marinades, karaage, stews and steamed dishes.
  • Soy sauce and miso: many brands contain a small amount of alcohol from fermentation or as a preservative.
  • Sweets: some cakes and chocolates contain rum or liqueur (洋酒, youshu).

Finding halal and Muslim-friendly food

  • Search “halal” in your map app in Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto, Sapporo and Fukuoka. You will find certified ramen, wagyu, yakiniku and Japanese set-meal restaurants.
  • Look for signs saying ハラール (haraaru), ハラル認証 (halal-certified) or “Muslim friendly”.
  • Major airports and some large stations and department stores have prayer rooms (礼拝室, reihaishitsu).
  • Seafood, vegetable and tofu dishes are a good fallback, but ask about mirin, sake and dashi.

Printable halal restaurant card

Show this card before ordering. It explains both pork and alcohol, which staff often do not connect with halal.

ハラール · HALAL

私はイスラム教徒（ムスリム）です。宗教上の理由で、次のものが食べられません。

I am Muslim. For religious reasons I cannot eat the following.

豚肉と、ラード・ポークエキス・ゼラチンなど豚由来のもの。

Pork and anything made from pork, such as lard, pork extract and gelatin.

お酒、みりん、料理酒、洋酒などアルコールが入ったもの。

Alcohol, including mirin, cooking sake and liqueur.

ハラールでない牛肉・鶏肉も食べられません。魚・野菜は大丈夫です。

I also cannot eat beef or chicken that is not halal. Fish and vegetables are fine.

豚肉に使った油や調理器具も使わないでください。

Please do not use oil or utensils that have been used for pork.

ご協力ありがとうございます。 Thank you for your help. · japanmust.com

Frequently asked questions

Is it easy to find halal food in Japan?

Easier every year. Tokyo, Osaka and Kyoto have halal-certified and Muslim-friendly restaurants, including ramen, yakiniku and wagyu places. Outside big cities options are thinner, so plan ahead.

Does Japanese food contain alcohol?

Often, in small amounts. Mirin (sweet rice wine) and cooking sake are standard in teriyaki sauce, simmered dishes, noodle broth and many marinades, and many soy sauces contain a little alcohol.

Is pork listed on Japanese food labels?

Pork (豚肉) is a recommended allergen item, not a mandatory one, so it is usually but not always shown. Look for 豚肉, ポーク, ラード (lard) and ゼラチン (gelatin) in the ingredient list.

Is sushi halal?

Fish and seafood are generally fine for many Muslim travelers, but sushi rice is seasoned with vinegar and sometimes mirin, and soy sauce may contain alcohol. Ask, or choose a halal-certified sushi shop.
